02 · Why it’s hard

Checking a battery independently is slow,
costly — or simply not possible from the outside.

A black box

The health figure is computed inside the car’s battery software. Outsiders see the answer, not how it was reached.

Lab tests are heavy

A true capacity test needs a full, controlled charge and discharge on lab equipment — impractical at every sale.

Evidence comes in pieces

Everyday charging is short and partial. Clean, complete measurements are rare — so any estimate rests on fragments.

03 · The insight
A health number is only worth
the evidence behind it.

So BatteryTruth measures two things at once: the battery’s capacity — and whether the data is strong enough to say so. When it isn’t, it says “not enough evidence” instead of guessing.

08 · Where it could matter

Anyone who buys, reuses or finances a battery
needs a health figure they can defend.

Potential applications · no deployments yet

Used-EV marketplaces & dealers

Price on proof, not trust: an evidence card attached to each listing.

Second-life repurposers

Triage used modules before reuse — the lab cells we studied were second-life cells.

Fleets & charging operators

Health screening during routine charging, no extra test cycle.

Insurers, lenders & warranties

Evidence for collateral value and claims, with stated limits.

Battery records & passports

Emerging battery-passport rules (e.g. the EU Battery Regulation) ask for battery-health information. An evidence report states what was measured, how confident it is, and what it cannot show.
